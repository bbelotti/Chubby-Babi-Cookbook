\recipe{Ryan's Mashed Potatoes}{Unsure which part of the process allowed my ex-roommate, Ryan Maxwell, to achieve such a consistently delicious result, I report below the preparation exactly as performed by him, lest the secret reside in his skillful execution. For best results, make this recipe using an instant pot or pressure cooker. Original recipe from \href{www.thesaltymarshmallow.com}{thesaltymarshmallow.com}}{}{}

\subsection*{Ingredients}
\begin{ingredients}
	\item 6 lb Idaho potatoes
	\item 4 tsp salt
	\item \sfrac{1}{2} cup butter
	\item \sfrac{1}{2} cup sour cream
	\item \sfrac{1}{2} cup milk
	\item 1 tsp garlic powder
	\item 1 tsp pepper
\end{ingredients}

\medskip

% --- Preparation ---
Peel and chop the \textbf{potatoes} into quarters.
Put the potatoes in the instant pot or pressure cooker, add the \textbf{salt} and add water until the potatoes are covered. 
Make sure that the ``maximum capacity'' line of your instant pot is abundantly surpassed. 
Pressure-cook the potatoes for 8 minutes\footnote{The instant pot is now basically a bomb.}\footnote{If you do not have an instant pot or pressure cooker, simply boil the potatoes in a pot until tender.}.
Once the potatoes are cooked, use the manual pressure release valve to spray the contents of the overfilled instant pot onto every exposed surface of your kitchen\footnote{This includes the ceiling.}\footnote{Use a single towel to protect your arm and hand from getting 3\textsuperscript{rd} degree burns.}.
Shower.
Drain the potatoes and return them to the instant pot. 
Eat something out of the trash can.
Add the \textbf{butter}, \textbf{milk}, \textbf{sour cream}, \textbf{garlic powder}, and \textbf{pepper}. 
Mash until you obtain a smooth, velvety texture. 
Enjoy these delicious mashed potatoes with your friends.
