\recipe{Calzonso's Birria}{For 6--8 servings. Refer to the \href{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2F5RO6vNSs}{recipe video by ``La Capital'' on YouTube}.}{}{}

\subsection*{Ingredients}
\begin{multicols}{2}
	\begin{ingredients}
	    \item 2 kg beef\footnote{Prefer meat with both fat and lean parts like chuck roast, short rib, oxtail, beef shanks.}
	    \item 2--3 marrow bones
	    \item 7 guajillo chiles\footnote{\label{note:chiles}Stemmed, seeded, and dried}
	    \item 5 ancho chiles\footref{note:chiles}
	    \item Árbol chile pods, as needed\footnote{This is what delivers the spiciness.}
	    \item 1 medium onion, quartered
	    \item 6 garlic cloves
	    \item 1 small (2 cm) piece of ginger
	    \item 1 teaspoon ground cumin
	    \item 1 teaspoon dried oregano
	    \item \sfrac{1}{2} teaspoon ground cloves
	    \item \sfrac{1}{2} teaspoon black pepper
	    \item 3 bay leaves
	    \item 1 small cinnamon stick
	    \item Love (non optional)
	    \item Morita chiles
	    \item Oaxaca cheese\footnote{If Oaxaca is not available, use mozzarella.}
	    \item Salt
	    \item Olive oil
	    \item Corn tortillas
	    \item Fresh cilantro and onion
	\end{ingredients}
\end{multicols}

\vspace{-0.5cm}
% --- Preparation ---

Add the \textbf{guajillo}, \textbf{ancho}, and \textbf{árbol chiles} to a pot and cover them with water. Bring the water to a boil, then turn off the heat and let the chiles rest for approximately 20 minutes. Drain and set aside.
In a dry skillet over low heat, gently toast the \textbf{garlic}, \textbf{ginger}, \textbf{cumin}, \textbf{oregano}, \textbf{cloves}, \textbf{black pepper}, and \textbf{cinnamon}. Toast each ingredient separately, as they require different cooking times. Be careful not to burn them.
Blend the softened \textbf{chiles}, \textbf{onion}, \textbf{garlic}, \textbf{ginger}, \textbf{cumin}, \textbf{oregano}, \textbf{cloves}, \textbf{black pepper}, and \textbf{cinnamon} until smooth.
Place the \textbf{beef} in a large pot and cover it with the \textbf{adobo} (the blended mix). Marinate the meat for at least 2 hours.

Add enough \textbf{water} to cover the meat, along with the \textbf{marrow bones} and \textbf{bay leaves}. Cook on low heat for 3--4 hours, or until the meat is tender. Only add the \textbf{salt} after the third hour.
Wash and dry the \textbf{morita chiles}. Heat \textbf{olive oil} in a small frying pan. Fry the chiles until they puff up, but do not let them burn. Allow them to cool, then add them to the broth along with the frying oil.
Once the birria is cooked, skim off the fat from the surface of the broth. Reserve the fat for cooking the quesadillas.

To prepare the quesadillas, dip a \textbf{corn tortilla} into the reserved birria fat and place it in a hot skillet. Add the \textbf{beef} and \textbf{cheese}, fold the tortilla, and cook until the cheese melts and the tortilla becomes slightly crisp.
Serve the birria with the quesadillas, chopped \textbf{cilantro}, and \textbf{onion}. Serve the broth separately in a cup.
